The practitioner

For the past four years I've built data systems that have to hold up in production, not just in a notebook — across healthcare, job-market data and university operations.

At the University of Maryland Counseling Center I built a no-show prediction model on HIPAA-regulated clinical records, wrote the DuckDB pipelines underneath it, and built the dashboards the counseling staff open every morning. At Canaria I ran anomaly detection over 15M+ job-market records and a salary model across 40,000+ US zip codes.

The tools change. What hasn't is the approach: understand the problem properly, be suspicious of your own data, and make the result something a person can act on.
